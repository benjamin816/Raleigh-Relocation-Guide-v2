Moving to Raleigh? Start With These 7 Areas

If you are moving from out of state, it is easy to start with Cary and spend the rest of your search comparing every house to it. But the better starting point is your week: where you will work, how often you will drive and what you want close to home.

Here are seven areas we would put on the map—and the practical tradeoff to think about in each.

Apex

A good place to compare newer communities with established resale neighborhoods. The western edge offers a different experience from the historic core: decide how much surrounding growth you want with your new home.

Holly Springs

Start around the NC-55 corridor to see how shopping, parks and neighborhoods connect. Look at 12 Oaks and Sunset Ridge for different home settings, then make sure the location fits the trips you will take most often.

Wake Forest

Compare Heritage and the newer communities on the east side if home space and outdoor time are priorities. Falls Lake and Joyner Park are part of the appeal; a daily trip across the Triangle may be the bigger compromise.

Rolesville

Think neighborhood recreation and a smaller-town setting. Granite Falls is one example from the video. Plan on comparing the home and amenities with the errands you would still drive into Wake Forest or Raleigh to handle.

North Raleigh

An established daily routine is the draw: shopping, parks and neighborhoods already in place. The house search often leans toward resale, so weigh home age and renovation needs alongside the convenience of the address.

Brier Creek

Put this on your list if RDU, RTP or Durham will feature heavily in your week. Compare the shopping around Brier Creek Commons with the surrounding townhomes, detached homes and golf-community options.

Cary

Still a useful benchmark for established neighborhoods and everyday convenience. Lochmere, Amberly and Preston show different sides of the town; Downtown Cary Park gives you a reason to explore the center, too.

You do not need to tour all seven. Pick two or three that fit your work route and preferred home style, then spend time doing ordinary things there: driving an errand, walking the neighborhood and seeing what feels convenient. That will tell you much more than a town name alone.
