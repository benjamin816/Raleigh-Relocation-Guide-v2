Moving to Raleigh? Start With These 7 Areas

If you're moving from out of state, you've probably heard a lot about the tried-and-true Raleigh suburbs like Cary, North Carolina. The mistake we see people make all the time is assuming the popular suburbs are automatically the right ones for them.

Here are seven different places to live around Raleigh that you might not have considered, or might not fully understand yet, but that could be a much better fit for the home, commute and ordinary daily life you actually want.

Apex: Newer Homes With West-Side Convenience

Apex draws many of the same families who begin their search in Cary, especially buyers who want strong everyday convenience with more chances to find a newer home. Western Apex and the Friendship area are growing quickly, while neighborhoods like Pearson Farms feel more established. Downtown Apex gives you a walkable historic center, and the Cary-Apex border keeps major shopping and recreation close.

Getting around: From central Apex, plan on roughly 20 to 30 minutes to downtown Raleigh and about 20 to 25 minutes to RDU in lighter traffic.

The honest downside: Growth is changing Apex fast. Road construction can shape the daily routine, and the popularity that makes Apex appealing keeps pushing prices higher.

Holly Springs: Parks, Community and Newer Homes

Holly Springs feels like a place where daily life can stay close to home. Ting Park, 12 Oaks, Sunset Ridge and the shopping along NC-55 create a rhythm of youth sports, neighborhood pools, errands and casual nights out. Relocators often choose it because they want planned neighborhoods, newer construction and a little more breathing room.

Getting around: Central Holly Springs is usually about 25 to 35 minutes from downtown Raleigh and about 20 to 30 minutes from RDU when traffic is moving.

The honest downside: The southern location can make RTP, Durham or North Raleigh commutes feel long, and NC-55 gets busy. Your work route matters just as much as the neighborhood.

Wake Forest: More Space, Nature and Its Own Identity

Wake Forest is popular with relocators who want larger homes, new construction and easy access to the outdoors. Heritage gives you an established neighborhood-and-shopping experience, newer communities keep growing on the east and north sides, and Falls Lake plus E. Carroll Joyner Park make weekend recreation part of ordinary life.

Getting around: The town describes downtown Raleigh as about 35 minutes away, while RDU is approximately 25 miles away. Your exact pocket can change the route quite a bit.

The honest downside: Wake Forest can feel farther away than it looks if your job or friends are on the western side of the Triangle. Downtown, Heritage and the developing edges also produce very different routines.

Rolesville: A Quieter Place With Room to Grow

Rolesville is one of those places people discover after they look beyond the best-known suburbs. Communities such as Granite Falls offer pools, trails and newer homes in a setting that still feels smaller and less built out. For buyers coming from a crowded metro, that quieter pace can feel like the reset they wanted.

Getting around: Rolesville is about 15 miles from downtown Raleigh, with town guidance estimating around 20 minutes downtown and about 30 minutes to RDU in normal conditions.

The honest downside: Shopping, dining and entertainment choices are still more limited. You may love the house and neighborhood while driving into Wake Forest or Raleigh for more errands and nights out.

North Raleigh: Established Neighborhoods Close to the City

North Raleigh works for people who want mature trees, established streets and quick access to the city without living downtown. Leesville, North Ridge and the neighborhoods around North Hills each offer a different version of that lifestyle, from quiet suburban pockets to shopping, dining and events.

Getting around: North Raleigh covers a broad area, so there is no honest single commute number. Depending on the neighborhood, downtown Raleigh may be 10 to 20 minutes away and RDU roughly 15 to 25 minutes away in lighter traffic.

The honest downside: Much of the housing stock is older. New construction often means premium-priced infill, so buyers may have to choose between the location they love and the turnkey home they pictured.

Brier Creek: Easy Access to RDU, RTP and Durham

Brier Creek is a practical favorite for people whose lives stretch across the Triangle. You are close to RDU, convenient to RTP and Durham, and surrounded by shopping, restaurants, townhomes, detached homes and golf-community living. For frequent travelers or households with jobs in different directions, that access can remove a surprising amount of friction from the week.

Getting around: RDU is often only 5 to 10 minutes away, while downtown Raleigh is roughly 20 to 25 minutes away in lighter traffic.

The honest downside: It can feel more like a convenient commercial hub than a charming hometown. If you dream about a walkable historic downtown, the location may work better than the atmosphere.

Cary: The Tried-and-True Favorite

Yes, Cary is the tried-and-true, arguably most popular suburb the Raleigh area has to offer. If you've done any research, you probably already know that people absolutely love it. Cary has established neighborhoods with strong amenities, and it is very family-friendly. Lochmere, Amberly and Preston are the kinds of neighborhoods where people move in and do not seem eager to leave.

Downtown Cary Park was recently transformed into an energetic gathering place for kids, friends, events and easy weekends. You are also close to major jobs, roughly 15 to 25 minutes from downtown Raleigh and often 10 to 20 minutes from RDU, depending on where in Cary you start.

The honest downside: The main complaints are crowding and price. Cary is one of the most premium-priced parts of our market, and buyers who want plentiful new construction without paying top dollar often skip it for other places on this list.

Still, Cary gets its strong reputation for a reason, and for some people it is the perfect fit.

Choose the Right Two or Three

If you're planning a trip or even just looking at the map, you do not need to tour all seven. Pick the two or three places that most closely align with your preferred home, your commute and what you love doing in ordinary daily life: running errands, walking with the kids, getting outside or hanging out with friends.
