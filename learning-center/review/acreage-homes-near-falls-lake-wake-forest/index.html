Wake Forest Acreage & New Construction

Acreage does not always mean giving up everyday convenience. West Wake Forest, near Falls Lake, offers a useful example: room for a custom home and outdoor projects, with shopping and major Triangle routes still within reach. The harder question is whether building from the ground up is the right way to get that space.

Space Without Feeling Cut Off

In the video, we explore a Drees Homes custom community on the west side of Wake Forest. The tour shows roughly one- to two-acre homesites—space that could suit a pool, a workshop or simply more distance between homes. Falls Lake is nearby for boating, fishing, biking and other outdoor time.

That setting is only part of the appeal. The drive also passes shopping around the US-1 corridor, including grocery stores, restaurants and everyday services. Routes toward downtown Raleigh, Durham, Research Triangle Park and RDU give buyers several directions to travel, though the actual commute depends on the address, time of day and route you take.

What Custom Really Changes

Drees showed multiple starting floor plans in the community, but the video makes clear that the finished homes need not look alike. Buyers could alter rooms, garages and finishes. That flexibility is exciting if the lot and layout are priorities. It also makes the final price less predictable than a completed home with a fixed specification.

When we filmed, we walked a model with generous living spaces, a large garage, guest accommodations and a primary suite designed around indoor-outdoor living. The most useful takeaway is not a particular finish: it is to decide which features are essential before changing the plan. Structural changes, design selections and the lot premium can move the budget materially.

Custom Build or Move-In-Ready Home?

The video compares a ground-up custom build with builder-selected, move-in-ready homes. A custom build offers more control, but requires time and decisions. A completed or nearly completed home gives up some personalization in exchange for a clearer product and potentially a shorter wait. Builder incentives and financing examples discussed in the video reflected the market when it was recorded; they are not current offers or guaranteed monthly payments.

Before choosing either route, ask for a written, current breakdown of base price, lot premium, design allowances, construction timing, HOA charges, taxes, insurance and any financing conditions. Compare that total with completed homes, not just the advertised starting price. Verify current inventory and incentives directly rather than relying on the video's snapshot.

Check the Address, Not Just the Area

The Wake County setting may matter to buyers thinking about schools, but assignments are property-specific and can change. Use Wake County's address lookup for the particular home. The same address-level check applies to utilities, setbacks, lot restrictions and whether your planned pool or outbuilding is actually permitted.

If acreage near Falls Lake is on your list, tour both a custom homesite and a finished home. Seeing the road, lot shape and everyday drive is the fastest way to learn which tradeoffs feel right for you.

At A Glance

Questions worth answering before you build:

  • How will you use the land?
  • Which plan changes are essential?
  • What is the all-in, current price?
  • Would a finished home meet the need sooner?
