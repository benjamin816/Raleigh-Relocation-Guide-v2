Raleigh or Durham? Start With How You Want to Live

919-737-7896LIVINGINRALEIGHTEAM@GMAIL.COM
YOUTUBE

If you’re moving to North Carolina, it’s easy to hear “Raleigh-Durham” and picture one place. Get in the car and drive through both, though, and you’ll start to see just how different they feel. Ricardo and I spend time in both cities, and the answer usually comes down to what you want your day to look like.

Start with a walk downtown

If you tell us you want to catch a game, walk to dinner and find a local spot afterward, downtown Durham is where we’d start. There’s a connected feel around the ballpark and American Tobacco, with DPAC nearby if you want to make a night of it.

Raleigh feels more spread out to us. You’ve got places like Glenwood South for a night out, but you’ll want to know which part of town you’re heading to. On your exploration trip, give both downtowns an evening. You’ll get a much better feel for what you like than you will from a list of places online.

In Durham, try the blocks around the Durham Bulls Athletic Park and American Tobacco before a show at DPAC. In Raleigh, give Glenwood South its own stop, then explore the downtown core separately.

Put your work address on the map

A lot of people call us already focused on Cary because they want to be close to Research Triangle Park and the airport. We get it. But before you settle on that search, take a look at the Durham side too, especially around southeast Durham and the 27703 ZIP code.

We’ve toured new-construction communities over there that put buyers close to the places they need to be during the week. If you want a newer home, that’s a comparison worth making alongside an established neighborhood on the Raleigh side. Drive from the actual community to your workplace at the time you’d leave.

A ZIP code is a starting point. Compare the particular home, your office entrance and the errands you’ll make on the way back. If you work from home, use that same exercise for the places you go several times a week.

Compare the whole neighborhood

Maybe you love the idea of moving into a neighborhood where the trees are mature and everything around you is already established. That’s a big part of what people like about the Raleigh neighborhoods we talk about in the video. Or maybe a brand-new home matters more to you, and you’re excited to see an area grow around it. That’s why we keep showing buyers communities on the Durham side too.

And if schools are part of your search, look beyond a city name or the idea that you have to be in one particular suburb. Start with the schools assigned to the address and what you’re looking for in a school. Then compare the home, the commute and the neighborhood together.

For the blog’s planning checklist, use Wake County’s address lookup when checking a Wake address. For a Durham address, start with Durham Public Schools. Make the address part of the comparison before you narrow down a home.
