Raleigh or Durham? Here’s How to Decide

919-737-7896LIVINGINRALEIGHTEAM@GMAIL.COM
YOUTUBE

If you’re moving to North Carolina, it’s easy to hear “Raleigh-Durham” and picture one place. Get in the car and drive through both, though, and you’ll start to see just how different they feel. Ricardo and I have spent a lot of time in both cities filming neighborhoods, brand-new communities, and everything in between. Here’s what you need to know.

Downtown

If you tell us you want to catch a game, walk to dinner, and find a local spot afterward, downtown Durham is where we’d start. Around the Durham Bulls ballpark, you’ve got restaurants, nearby shops, and the American Tobacco District, where old tobacco warehouses have been turned into places to eat, work, and hang out. You can see that history in the brick buildings around you. You also have DPAC, the Durham Performing Arts Center, nearby for Broadway shows, concerts, and comedy. It makes it easy to build a whole evening around one part of town.

Raleigh, on the other hand, feels more spread out to us. If you ask where we’d start for a night out, we’d point you toward Glenwood South. That’s where you’ll find a lot of the bars, busy patios, and late-night party scene. Durham feels a little more artsy and centered around local food and shops; Glenwood South leans more toward a high-energy night out. That’s the difference we’d want you to see for yourself.

What we’d recommend is checking out both downtowns and seeing which one you like. But most people we work with aren’t going to live downtown. It’s one part of the conversation. The next question is where you’ll actually spend your workweek.

In Durham, the American Tobacco Campus gives those old industrial buildings a new life, right beside the ballpark and DPAC. In Raleigh, Glenwood South is its own nightlife district. It gives you a different evening from the quieter blocks around the Capitol.

Your commute

A lot of people are already focused on Raleigh because they want to be close to Research Triangle Park and the airport. Suburbs like Cary and Morrisville put you close to RTP, where you’ll find major employers like Cisco, Lenovo, and IBM. We get why those areas are at the top of the list.

But before you settle on that, also consider the Durham side, especially southeast Durham and the 27703 ZIP code. We’ve toured a lot of new-construction communities over there. You can find brand-new homes in an area that still has room to grow, with RTP and the airport nearby. That’s a different option from some of the more built-out parts of Cary, Morrisville, and North Raleigh.

If you want more home or a newer home without giving up that access to the jobs, this is a part of the Triangle we’d want on your list. You don’t have to choose Cary just because you work in RTP. Southeast Durham gives you another way to stay close to work and the airport while getting the kind of home you actually want.

For an example of the new homes we’re talking about, Triple Crown is a new-construction community in 27703 that highlights its proximity to RTP. The point is to compare that side of the Triangle alongside the Raleigh suburbs, especially if a brand-new home is high on your list.

Your neighborhood

Maybe you love the idea of moving into a neighborhood where the trees are mature and everything around you is already established. That’s a big part of what people like about the Raleigh neighborhoods we talk about in the video. Or maybe a brand-new home matters more to you, and you’re excited to see an area grow around it. That’s why we keep showing buyers communities on the Durham side too.

And if schools are part of your search, look beyond a city name or the idea that you have to be in one particular suburb. Start with the schools assigned to the address and what you’re looking for in a school. Then compare the home, the commute and the neighborhood together.

For the blog’s planning checklist, use Wake County’s address lookup when checking a Wake address. For a Durham address, start with Durham Public Schools. Make the address part of the comparison before you narrow down a home.
