Which Side of Raleigh Fits Your Daily Life?

919-737-7896LIVINGINRALEIGHTEAM@GMAIL.COM
YOUTUBE

If you’re moving to Raleigh, North Carolina, and trying to figure out where you should call home, here’s where we’d start. Get in the car and give yourself a weekend to drive around. The north, west, east and south sides of Raleigh and its suburbs start to feel very, very different once you’re out there. Chances are one or two will appeal to you a lot more than the others.

West Raleigh

Cary, Apex, Morrisville and Holly Springs are some of the first places people ask us about when they’re moving here. Cary gets a lot of attention, and once you drive around the west side, you can see why. Research Triangle Park, RDU and downtown Raleigh are all within reach, and you’ve got established master-planned communities, parks, schools, and plenty of places to shop and eat. The whole area has a polished, planned-out feel.

The tradeoff is that some of the most sought-after parts of Cary can come with a higher price tag. It can feel a little crammed at times, and if you want a brand-new home, you may find more to look at as you get farther out.

If you’re touring the west side, make a stop at Fenton in Cary, then drive through an established neighborhood and compare it with one in Apex or Holly Springs. If you’ll be driving into RTP, try the route from the actual house at the time you’d leave for work.

North Raleigh

Ricardo and I have both called the north side home, and this is the part we show people who come to town thinking it’s West Cary or bust. Brier Creek and North Hills both give you plenty of shopping and things to do, and a lot of established North Raleigh neighborhoods have that same feel people love about the west side.

Then you’ve got Wake Forest. This is where we’d take you if you want to compare new construction at different price points, from production builders to the luxury custom homes we’ve featured on the channel. And if what you really want is a bigger yard, go a little farther toward Falls Lake. You can often get more house or yard for your budget on the north side, and many addresses are still in the Wake County school system.

Brier Creek makes sense as its own stop if the airport is a big part of your week. Give Wake Forest a separate visit so you can see how much of your shopping, dining and time outdoors you can do close to home. Check the assigned schools for any specific address you’re considering.

East Raleigh

We’re seeing more buyers take a serious look at the east side because they want new construction and a little more room. Wendell and Knightdale are the first two places we’d have you drive through. If you’ve been looking for a custom home or space for a bigger lot, put Zebulon on your list too.

If you haven’t seen our Wendell Falls walking tour, start there. It’ll give you a good idea of what’s happening on the east side and what you get in a master-planned community out here. The nice part is you can still get into downtown Raleigh from the closer-in communities. If you’re driving across town to RTP or the airport, the route can take longer depending on where you start and when you leave.

Try Knightdale Station Park and Wendell Falls on the same trip. If you decide to keep going out toward Zebulon, drive back to the places you’d actually go every week. That’s when you’ll know whether the extra space is worth the extra drive.

South Raleigh

Maybe what you really want is enough yard to spread out. South Raleigh is worth a drive for that alone. If you still need to be close to downtown, start in Garner and see what you can get in a newer community. Then head toward Clayton and check out Flowers Plantation, where you can pair a new-construction search with a stop at the shopping center right in the community.

Once you get into parts of unincorporated Wake County south of Raleigh, you can find custom homes on bigger lots and still have your everyday errands within reach. Plus, we’ve got a Costco down here on Fayetteville Road.

Put downtown Clayton and Flowers Plantation on your map separately. They’re two different stops. The south side can make a lot of sense if you work downtown or from home. If you’ll be driving to RTP several days a week, run that route before you settle on a house.
